# What is an ad's job — an inbox or a sale?

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Parent: ../map.md

## Question

The market teardown found the category's funnel is **ad → Messenger/Zalo inbox → consult**,
not ad → checkout, and that the product is sold as a *liệu trình* rather than a unit. The
canon's structures mostly end in a close — *Action*, *Push*, *Response*, *Purchase*.

Confirm what Cambridge's ads are actually asked to do, and decide what that makes the final
beat. If the ad's job is to earn a conversation, the close is a qualification device, not a
sale — which changes the CTA catalogue, what an ad may promise, how much of the offer
belongs in the ad at all, and how a variation is judged good.

Also settle whether one doctrine covers every ad, or whether the pipeline must distinguish
ads by job (earn an inbox / re-engage a lapsed lead / defend against a competitor / build
the category) — because a single close beat cannot serve all of them.

Ask the operator; this is a business fact plus a decision, not something research settles.

## Answer

**The ad's job is to earn a Messenger conversation with a consultant — one destination, no
exceptions.** Confirmed by the operator, and it matches what the pipeline already asserts:
`ad/platform-constraints` fixes the CTA destination as a human consultant via Messenger,
and `ssc-ads-writer` states outright that ads are optimized for conversion while posts earn
conversation. Zalo, hotline and landing pages are **not** conversion destinations for ads —
the brand footer's hotline is identifying information, not a second route (and
`rules/organic-vs-paid-firewall` bans soliciting the reader's number on paid).

**The close does two jobs at once: qualify AND pre-sell.** Not volume-maximising. The ad
carries enough of the argument — mechanism, proof, what the programme actually is — that the
reader arrives at the inbox already persuaded, and the close names who this is for and what
happens next, so the wrong reader is deterred rather than harvested. Consequences the later
tickets must honour:

- The canon's terminal beats (*Action*, *Push*, *Response*, *Purchase*) are **rewritten as
  qualified invitation**, never as a purchase ask. Curiosity-gap and urgency devices whose
  only function is to maximise clicks are off-doctrine here, because they import unqualified
  readers into a human consultant's time.
- Pre-selling raises the proof and mechanism load carried by the ad itself — which collides
  head-on with what [The proof problem](./12-proof-problem.md) has left legal, and with the
  fold constraint on length. That collision is real and belongs to
  [the copy application table](./07-copy-application-table.md).
- "Good" is therefore not inbox volume. A variation that earns fewer but better-qualified
  conversations beats one that earns more — which the writer's self-score does not currently
  measure at all.

**Deferred, not decided**: whether the L1 / L2(Empathy|Science|Solution|SocialProof) / L3
layer taxonomy remains the way ad jobs are named. It may be redundant with awareness stage
or with lead type once the spine exists. Split out as
[Is the layer taxonomy redundant?](./15-layer-vs-spine.md).
