# The opening beat vs Meta's Personal Attributes policy

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 12
Parent: ../map.md

## Question

Meta's Personal Attributes policy bans second-person assertions about a reader's body or
health — which forbids, applied literally to weight, the opening move of nearly every
problem-led flow in the canon: PAS's *Problem*, BAB's *Before*, the VSL's early
agitation steps. The Vietnamese market compounds it: platform banned-word lists flag
*giảm cân / béo / mập / gầy*, and practitioners have already drifted to *kiểm soát cân nặng*.

The category's own dominant hook — the pain-mirror, *chạm đúng nỗi đau* — is exactly the
move the policy forbids in second person.

Decide what our opening beat is allowed to be, and what it should be. Options the evidence
puts on the table: first-person or third-person framing (a story about someone, not an
assertion about the reader); the situation rather than the body; RMBC's
**problem-mechanism** — explaining *why* the thing fails, which persuades without asserting
anything about the reader or promising an outcome.

Output: the opening-beat rule the writer works to, the phrasings that are refused outright,
and how the ~125-character fold interacts with it (the canon sweep's conclusion was that
the fold argues for a **first-beat rule**, not for choosing a framework).

## Answer

**The rule is about grammatical person, not vocabulary.** `rules/banned-words` already handles
the lexical half — it substitutes for *béo phì / mỡ thừa / bụng mỡ*, bans *đốt mỡ*, "giảm X kg
trong Y ngày", medical verbs and fake scarcity. What no existing doc covers, and what Meta's
Personal Attributes policy actually polices, is **asserting something about the reader**.

So the doctrine adds one line the vocabulary list cannot express:

> **Never assert a personal attribute of the reader.** Addressing her is fine — *chị* is the
> brand's register and stays. Claiming to know something about her body, health, weight or
> failures is not: *"Chị đang thừa cân?"*, *"Bạn đã thử giảm cân nhiều lần mà thất bại?"* are
> refused however gently phrased. The test is grammatical, and it is checkable: a
> second-person sentence whose predicate is a body/health/failure state fails, regardless of
> the words used.

This is what makes the category's own dominant hook — the pain-mirror, *chạm đúng nỗi đau* —
unavailable **in the form everyone else writes it**, and it is why nearly every problem-led
flow's literal opening (PAS's *Problem*, BAB's *Before*, the VSL's agitation steps) has to be
re-framed rather than adopted.

**Four permitted opening frames — the pool a batch's lead types draw from.** All four are
adopted; each reaches the same recognition without asserting anything about the reader:

1. **Third-person story** — it happened to someone else, and she recognises herself in it.
   This is the compliant form of the pain-mirror, and it carries the Story lead.
2. **First-person** — the founder or a consultant speaking about her own experience. The
   speaker asserts about *herself*, which the policy does not touch; it also aligns with the
   human proof already adopted ([The proof problem](./12-proof-problem.md)).
3. **Situation, not body** — a shared circumstance (the office lunch, the family meal, the
   1am silence) rather than a personal attribute. Hooks on recognition, asserts nothing.
4. **Mechanism-first** — open on the explanation itself: why the thing fails, why the body
   responds as it does. Asserts nothing about anyone, and the category has none of it.

**The fold is governed by the lead, not by a fixed rule.** An Offer lead and a Story lead do
not have the same first move, so no single prescription ("land the recognition" / "a complete
miniature") is imposed. What is checked instead: **nothing essential may be stranded below the
fold** — if the copy only makes sense once expanded, it fails. The ~125-character truncation is
therefore a *diagnostic applied per lead*, not a template. Concrete lengths and per-lead
first-move guidance belong to
[the copy application table](./07-copy-application-table.md).

**Note for the KB.** The banned-words doc is the natural home for the person rule, but it is
structured as a word-substitution table and this rule is grammatical. Where it lands —
extending that doc, or a new one — belongs to
[Where the doctrine lives](./03-doctrine-home.md).

## Amendment — 2026-07-30, after [Reach the actual ads](./11-observe-real-ads.md)

Observed live, active, high-impression VN ads open with exactly the assertion this ticket
forbids (*"Bạn đang mệt mỏi vì ăn kiêng khắc nghiệt mà cân vẫn không giảm?"*, *"Vòng 1 nhỏ –
kém săn chắc – thiếu tự tin khi mặc đồ ôm?"*). **Meta's Personal Attributes policy is not
reliably enforced for Vietnamese weight-loss ads.**

The decision stands unchanged — the four permitted frames carry no rejection risk, no legal
exposure, and they fit a voice built on not pressuring the reader. But the *reasoning* changes:
this is a **risk position deliberately taken**, not a constraint every competitor faces. The
doctrine must state that plainly, because anyone who opens the Ad Library will otherwise
conclude the rule is misinformed and quietly stop following it.

Related: the category also evades text detection by breaking banned words with punctuation
(`G.iảm C.ân`, `m.ỡ`). That is a **refused device** — a broken word is still the claim.
