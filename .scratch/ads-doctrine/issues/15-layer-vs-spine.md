# Is the layer taxonomy redundant with the spine?

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 04
Parent: ../map.md

## Question

Every brief today declares a `target_layer_term_id` — L1, one of four L2 slots (Empathy /
Science / Solution / SocialProof), L3, or YouTube — and `ad/creative-guidelines` §2–3 makes
that layer, and only that layer, decide the CTA family and the overall tone: L1 and L3 must
close on an explicit *nhắn tin*; no L2 slot may carry a messaging or sales CTA; YouTube
points at the Fanpage. Persona, route and awareness stage decide hook and body; layer
decides the close.

That is a taxonomy of ad *jobs* sitting alongside a taxonomy of readers — and once the spine
exists it may be saying the same thing twice. Awareness stage already implies how ready the
reader is to be asked for something; lead type (if the spine routes by lead) implies the
opening; both bear on what the close may ask.

Decide: does the layer survive as a first-class axis a brief must declare, does it collapse
into the spine's own axes, or does it demote to a pure media-buying label with no say over
copy at all? If it survives, say what it means that awareness stage does not already say,
and restate its rule in the spine's terms rather than as a CTA phrasing table.

Constraint from [What is an ad's job](./13-what-the-ad-is-for.md): whatever survives, every
ad's destination is the Messenger consultation, and every close both qualifies and
pre-sells. A layer that forbids a messaging CTA is therefore forbidding the ad's only
conversion route — decide deliberately whether that is intended (a genuine awareness-building
tier) or an artefact.

## Answer

**The layer survives as a first-class axis, and it is not redundant — because it was never a
copy taxonomy.** Reading `ad/creative-guidelines` §2 live settles it: the layer is a **funnel
role with its own KPI**.

- **L1 = QUALIFIER.** Cold. Filters rather than hard-sells; scored on cost-per-purchase, and
  expected to run roughly 2× warm.
- **L2 = FEEDER.** Builds the warm pool L3 harvests; scored on **reach / CPM / ThruPlay, never
  on orders**. A weak feeder makes L3 expensive.
- **L3 = CLOSER.** The cheapest orders in the account, and where the strongest proof and
  reassurance belong.

**So it answers a different question from awareness stage, and the brief declares both.**
Layer says *where the ad sits in the account and how it will be judged*; awareness stage says
*what the reader already knows*, which is what routes the lead type. They correlate — L1 skews
cold, L3 warm — but they are not the same: a cold L1 audience still contains product-aware
readers, and it is precisely holding both that lets an L2 feeder ad carry a solution-aware
lead while still refusing a messaging CTA.

**Restated in the spine's terms**, replacing the CTA-phrasing-table framing:

| Axis | Question it answers | Governs |
|---|---|---|
| **Layer** | Where in the funnel, and scored how? | The close's *job*, the tone, and which KPI judges the ad |
| **Awareness stage** | What does she already know? | Which lead types are admissible |
| **Persona × route** | Who, and via what appeal? | Hook and body content |
| **Mechanism** | Why does this work / fail? | The mandatory proof spine |

**This corrects one line in [What is an ad's job](./13-what-the-ad-is-for.md).** That ticket
concluded every close both qualifies and pre-sells. That holds for **L1 and L3**, whose
destination is the Messenger consultation. It does **not** hold for L2: an L2 ad's job is to
be *seen* by the right pool, and the doc forbids it any messaging or sales CTA — soft
engagement only, across all four voice slots. So the rule is **per layer**:

- **L1** — qualify hard (it is the filter), pre-sell lightly.
- **L2** — neither. Earn attention and recognition; the close is a soft engagement, and scoring
  it by orders is the documented way to kill the wrong ad set.
- **L3** — pre-sell hard (strongest proof lives here), qualify gently; the reader has already
  met the brand.

**An observation the doctrine should carry forward, not resolve here.** §4.3 already mandates
in-batch diversity, but expressed as *hook formula* and *entry point* rather than the four
coverage axes settled in [What makes N variations different](./08-variation-mechanics.md), and
§2's "3–4 independent angles per ad set" is flagged in the doc itself as team heuristic with
**no first-party research record** — consistent with what the canon sweep found about the whole
creative-count genre. When the doctrine is written, §4.3's diversity rule and the coverage axes
must be reconciled into one statement rather than left as two overlapping ones.

`brand/angles` §5 contradicts it and that this doc wins until `brand/angles` is updated. That
inconsistency is live now, independent of this map.
