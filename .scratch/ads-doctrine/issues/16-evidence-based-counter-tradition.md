# Does evidence-based marketing get a say?

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Parent: ../map.md

## Question

[The framework spine](./04-framework-spine.md) is built on the direct-response tradition, and
adopted knowingly without efficacy evidence — none exists for any framework in the field. The
one body of third-party-auditable evidence that does exist (Ehrenberg-Bass; Binet & Field)
argues against DR's premises: that mental availability and broad reach beat targeted
persuasion, that most buyers are light and infrequent, that distinctive assets do more work
than arguments, and that the long-term effect of brand-building outweighs short-term
activation at a roughly 60/40 split.

Cambridge Diet Vietnam is a considered, high-price, consultation-sold *liệu trình* in a
category whose trust has been publicly broken — which is not the fast-moving-consumer-goods
setting most of that evidence comes from. So the question is not "who is right" but what, if
anything, this tradition changes here.

Decide whether it: tempers the ads doctrine (e.g. consistency and distinctive assets across
ads become a rule, not a preference); belongs one level up as a strategy-layer concern about
the reach/persuasion split and what share of spend builds the brand; or is genuinely out of
scope for an effort about ad structure and copy.

If it earns a place, say concretely what it obliges — a rule a skill could actually check, not
a philosophy paragraph.

## Answer

**It earns a place — and it turns out the account already implements most of it without naming
it.** `ad/creative-guidelines` §2 runs L2 as a feeder judged on reach/CPM and **never** on
orders; §6.2 records person-led creative winning reach at CPM under 10k while heritage/EU
messaging at awareness cost 33–43k; §1 asserts that under Advantage+ broad the *creative* does
the audience filtering rather than interest targeting. That is mental availability, built by
consistent recognisable assets, funded as its own tier — arrived at from the account's own
numbers rather than from the literature.

So the tradition is not adopted as a philosophy or a spend ratio. It is adopted as **three rules
a skill can actually check**, each of which draws a boundary the direct-response half of the
doctrine would otherwise cross:

**1. Distinctive-asset consistency across ads.** The brand's recognisable identifiers — Kiều My
herself, the specialist-on-Zalo motif, the visual signature — **recur** rather than being varied
for novelty. This is the necessary counterweight to the coverage axes: **coverage varies the
argument, never the identifiers.** Without this rule, "genuinely different options"
([08](./08-variation-mechanics.md)) would licence exactly the churn that destroys recognition —
and the account's own §6.2 evidence says the person-led core is what earns cheap reach. Concretely:
a batch may span four leads, four proof devices and three registers while every asset still
reads unmistakably as this brand.

**2. Never score a feeder by conversion.** Already in §2, but **promoted from a layer note to
doctrine**, because it is the single most consequential misreading available: an L2 ad set with
no orders looks like a failure and is not one, and killing it makes L3 expensive — the doc says
so directly. Stated as doctrine, it also protects the map's own conclusion that "good" means a
better-qualified conversation ([13](./13-what-the-ad-is-for.md)) from being applied to a tier
whose job is not conversation at all ([15](./15-layer-vs-spine.md)).

**3. Reach breadth over narrow targeting.** The audience is not narrowed to the persona the angle
addresses; the creative filters. This codifies *why* persona lives in the creative rather than in
the targeting, which is otherwise the most tempting thing to get wrong once briefs are explicitly
persona-tagged — a persona-tagged brief invites a persona-narrowed audience, and under Advantage+
broad that is a mistake. It also explains a rule already in §4.3 that would otherwise look
arbitrary: L1 ad sets hold one persona for **signal consistency**, and persona breadth is achieved
*between* ad sets, not by narrowing the audience of each.

**What is NOT adopted.** No spend split (the 60/40 long/short heuristic is FMCG-derived and this
is a considered, consultation-sold *liệu trình*); no claim that persuasion doesn't work; no
reach-at-all-costs posture. The disagreement is recorded rather than resolved: this tradition is
the only third-party-auditable evidence in the field and it disputes the direct-response premises
the rest of the doctrine is built on. The doctrine says that plainly — as
[04](./04-framework-spine.md) requires — and takes the three rules that survive the difference in
category.

**Where it lands**: rules 2 and 3 are restatements of existing `ad/creative-guidelines` content
promoted to doctrine status, so they belong in the new spine doc with pointers back. Rule 1 is
genuinely new and needs a home alongside the coverage axes, since it is the constraint *on* them
([03](./03-doctrine-home.md)).
