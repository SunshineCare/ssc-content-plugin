## 0. How to read this

### 0.1 The three classes the ticket asks for

- **STRUCTURE** — how a piece is assembled. Ordered beats. Answers "what goes where."
- **DIAGNOSTIC** — which structure to pick, or which part is broken. Answers "what is this
  situation / what failed."
- **GENERATOR** — how to find something to say. Answers "what is the subject."

Most named "frameworks" in the field are structures. **Generators are scarce and
diagnostics are scarcer still** — that asymmetry is itself the most decision-relevant
finding in this sweep, because the map's stated driver ("copy comes out bland and
same-shaped") is a *generator* problem, and the canon's centre of mass is structures.

### 0.2 Source-trust tiers used below

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Originator's own book / site / course, verified on a primary URL; or a first-party platform policy/doc page |
| **B** | Respected practitioner or reference work restating it, originator identifiable |
| **C** | Widely repeated; no identifiable originator; aggregator pages only |
| **D** | Folklore — no traceable origin at all |

Several things this pipeline might reasonably have assumed are **C or D**. Those are called
out in §9.

### 0.3 What could not be fetched

- *Breakthrough Advertising* and *Great Leads* are print-only; the strongest available
  sources are the publisher page plus long-form practitioner notes that quote verbatim.
- Meta's `facebook.com/business/help/*` pages are client-rendered and return title-only on
  fetch. Claims from them are marked **[snippet-only]** and corroborated through Jon Loomer
  or Meta newsroom pages where possible. `transparency.meta.com` and
  `facebook.com/business/news/*` fetched cleanly and are quoted directly.

## 1. The routers — diagnostics that sit above everything

### 1.1 Eugene Schwartz — awareness × market sophistication (*Breakthrough Advertising*, 1966)

**Tier A/B.** Publisher: [breakthroughadvertisingbook.com](https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/breakthrough-advertising-book/).
Verbatim-quoting notes: [Valchanova](https://valchanova.me/breakthrough-advertising-copywriting-book-review/),
[Mirasee](https://mirasee.com/blog/eugene-schwartz-breakthrough-advertising/),
[Orendorff on mass desire](https://medium.com/@iconiContent/mass-desire-the-first-unbreakable-law-of-breakthrough-copywriting-bf7b66fe344e).

**What it claims.** Copy cannot create desire; it can only "take the hopes, dreams, fears and
desires that already exist in the hearts of millions of people, and focus those
already-existing desires onto a particular product." The copywriter's job is "not to create
this mass desire — but to channel and direct it." Mass desire is produced by **mass instinct**
and **mass problem**, modulated by **trend** and **mass education** (the market keeps getting
smarter).

**Axis 1 — the five awareness stages** (one buyer's knowledge). Schwartz's own operative
rule: *if the prospect is aware of your product, the headline starts with the product; if he
is not aware, the headline starts with the desire.*

| Stage | Definition | What the opening must do |
|---|---|---|
| Most Aware | Knows the product, wants it, hasn't bought | Name + price/offer. The offer *is* the ad |
| Product Aware | Knows it exists, not convinced it performs | New proof, sharpened image, or a new mechanism/benefit |
| Solution Aware | Knows the outcome wanted, doesn't know you deliver it | Crystallise the desire *and* its solution so he recognises it |
| Problem Aware | Feels the problem, doesn't know a solution exists | Name the need, dramatise it, then present the product as inevitable |
| Unaware | Doesn't recognise the desire, or it "resists being summed up" | Don't sell — echo the emotion, build identification |

Copyhackers' one deep treatment (Problem-Aware) agrees, and adds the length corollary: a page
must be "as long as is necessary to make the argument that will address the prospect in their
state of awareness" ([Copyhackers on long pages](https://copyhackers.com/2014/07/long-pages/),
[Copyhackers on problem-aware pages](https://copyhackers.com/2020/07/how-to-write-a-landing-page-examples/)).

**Axis 2 — the five sophistication stages** (how many times the *whole market* has already
heard your claim).

| Stage | Market condition | Prescribed move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nobody has made this claim | State it plainly. "Be simple. Be direct. Above all, don't be fancy" |
| 2 | Competitors copied the claim | Enlarge/amplify the *same* claim. Schwartz explicitly **withholds** mechanism and identification here |
| 3 | Claims exhausted, disbelief | Introduce a **new mechanism** — "a new way to make the old promise work" |
| 4 | Mechanism copied | **Elaborate/expand** the mechanism — easier, quicker, surer |
| 5 | Everything exhausted and distrusted | Abandon promise-selling → **identification**: prestige, role, belonging |

Worked example on a weight-loss category (directly analogous to a meal-replacement brand):
plain claim → "twice the weight in half the time" → "a new fat-blocking enzyme" →
"second-generation enzyme, no side effects" → "join the people who eat like this"
([Selfstorming — C tier](https://www.selfstorming.com/tools/libraries/frameworks/market-sophistication)).

**Required inputs.** A mass-desire diagnosis; a **competitor claim history** (sophistication
cannot be read without one); an awareness read of the segment; a product-capability inventory
grouped by *which desire each feature satisfies*; trend monitoring.

**Output.** Not copy. A positioning decision for the opening: which desire is being channelled,
where the headline may start, and **what type of claim is legal** (plain / enlarged /
mechanism / better mechanism / identity).

**Failure modes.**
- *Treating awareness as a linear funnel and siloing campaigns by it.* The sharpest
  paid-social criticism: a single ad set contains all five stages at once, so awareness is a
  lens for writing the message, **not a campaign structure**
  ([Selfstorming — C tier](https://www.selfstorming.com/tools/libraries/frameworks/customer-awareness-stages)).
  Post-Advantage+ you cannot target an awareness stage anyway.
- *Non-linearity.* People skip and revert; the model describes states, not a path
  ([mxmoritz](https://www.mxmoritz.com/article/stages-of-awareness)).
- *Sophistication is judged, not measured.* Two competent practitioners can call the same
  market 3 vs 4 and the whole prescription flips.
- *Mechanism-escalation trap* — inventing "new mechanisms" for a market that is actually at
  stage 5 and needs claims dropped.
- **Honest gap: no high-trust, evidence-based critique of Schwartz exists.** No academic or
  Ehrenberg-Bass-style test of the sophistication ladder was found. The criticisms above are
  practitioner craft-heuristics on C-tier pages.

**Classification: DIAGNOSTIC (dominant), with a weak GENERATOR edge.** It selects which
structure/claim-type is legal; it hands you no skeleton for the body. The generator edge is
real but secondary — mass-desire analysis, the stage-3 demand for a *new mechanism*, and the
stage-5 identity pivot all push you to go find something.

**Paid-social length implication.** None directly, but via Copyhackers: length is a function
of awareness, not a universal rule. Cold/unaware → more room needed; most-aware → an offer
line suffices.

### 1.2 The six-lead taxonomy — **Masterson & Forde, not Makepeace**

**Attribution correction (the ticket is wrong, and cleanly so).** The six-lead taxonomy is
**Michael Masterson & John Forde, *Great Leads: The Six Easiest Ways to Start Any Sales
Message*** (AWAI, 2011/2012)
([Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Great-Leads-Easiest-Start-Message/dp/0982150032),
[AWAI launch](https://www.awai.com/2012/01/just-released-great-leads/)). "Michael Masterson"
is a pen name of Mark Ford ([AWAI bio](https://www.awai.com/bio/michael-masterson/)).

**Clayton Makepeace did not author it.** He appears *inside* the book as a cited authority —
for the observation that dominant-emotion leads increasingly beat benefit-driven headlines in
testing ([book text](https://vdoc.pub/documents/great-leads-the-six-easiest-ways-to-start-any-sales-message-66em2alhuei0)).
His own body of work (the "Makepeace Method", his long-form outline/checklists) is a full
promo architecture and an emotion-first doctrine, **not** a six-lead taxonomy
([AWAI](https://www.awai.com/makepeace/copywriting-business/learn/blueprint/)).
**Zero sources** support the Makepeace attribution. Any doctrine doc should say
*Masterson & Forde (2011)*.

**What it claims.** The "lead" is the first **100–600 words**; roughly 80% of an ad's success
is attributed to headline + lead (an AWAI marketing claim, **no independent study found**).
The six sit on a **direct→indirect spectrum**, and awareness sets your position on it.

| Lead | What it is | Prescribed for |
|---|---|---|
| **Offer** (most direct) | Opens with the deal — price, guarantee, premium | Most Aware |
| **Promise** | Opens with the single biggest claim | Product Aware (+ direct edge of Solution Aware) |
| **Problem–Solution** | Charged problem first; product revealed late | Solution Aware (+ direct edge of Problem Aware) |
| **Big Secret** | Teases hard-to-get knowledge / a formula | Problem Aware |
| **Proclamation** (indirect) | Shocking statement, incredible fact, bold prediction | Unaware |
| **Story** (indirect) | Narrative — origin, customer, biography, track record | Unaware and Problem Aware; also the skeptic's lead at any level |

```
Most Aware      → Offer
Product Aware   → Offer or Promise
Solution Aware  → Promise or Problem-Solution
Problem Aware   → Problem-Solution, Big Secret, or Story
Unaware         → Proclamation or Story
```

Governing monotone: **directness decreases as awareness decreases.** Note the mapping is
**overlapping, not one-to-one** — which is a feature (room for a creative choice) and a hazard
(not deterministic enough to automate blindly).

**This is the only awareness→X mapping in the canon with a primary source. It maps awareness
to a LEAD TYPE, not to a copy formula.** See §7.1 — that distinction decides the operator's
first claim.

**Inputs.** An awareness diagnosis (i.e. Schwartz's *output* is this framework's *input* — the
two are chained, not parallel); the offer; the big idea; emotional research; proof/story assets.

**Output.** The opening 100–600 words + its headline, tagged with one declared lead type.

**Failure modes.** Awareness misdiagnosis propagates straight through with no independent
check; mixed-awareness audiences break a single-lead commitment (hence: produce a *spread* of
lead types per subject and let delivery sort them); overlap makes it non-deterministic; the
indirect leads (Proclamation, Big Secret) are exactly the shapes that attract health-claim
scrutiny; the 100–600-word budget is direct-mail native and does not survive compression to a
Meta primary text — the *shapes* survive, the word budgets and "delay the product 400 words"
mechanics do not. Evidence base is practitioner testimony; AWAI is publisher and promoter.

**Classification: DIAGNOSTIC + STRUCTURE hybrid, mostly STRUCTURE at point of use.** The
diagnostic half is thin — it borrows its entire input axis from Schwartz. It is **not** a
generator: it presumes the big idea, the offer and the emotional research already exist.

### 1.3 ACCA / DAGMAR — Awareness, Comprehension, Conviction, Action

**Tier B, and better sourced than the copywriting blogs suggest.** ACCA is **not** a
copywriter's invention: it is **DAGMAR** — **Russell H. Colley**, *Defining Advertising Goals
for Measured Advertising Results*, Association of National Advertisers, **1961**
([hierarchy-of-effects survey PDF](https://repository.bakrie.ac.id/162/1/KOM-Artikel-009%20Hierarchy%20of%20effects%20model%20in%20advertising%20with%20cover.pdf),
[CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/learn/resources/management/hierarchy-of-effects)).
Its 1961 sibling is Lavidge & Steiner's six-step hierarchy. **Bob Bly catalogued it, did not
originate it** ([The Copywriter's Handbook](https://www.bly.com/copyhandbook/)). Copyhackers'
"used particularly for non-profits" note is a Copyhackers observation, not Colley.

**Why it matters here.** ACCA carries the **DAGMAR measurement premise**: each stage is a
*measurable communications objective*, so you set the ad's goal at **one** stage and measure
that stage. That is a different posture from "write in this order," and it makes ACCA the most
measurement-aligned framework in the field.

**Where it beats AIDA.** When the product is **unfamiliar or misunderstood** — new category,
novel mechanism, or a category the audience holds an incorrect model of. AIDA presupposes the
reader understands what's being sold and only needs to want it; ACCA is for when wanting is
blocked by not-understanding.

**Inputs.** A definition parseable by a naive reader; the specific *misunderstanding* to
correct; proof; an action. **Output.** Explanatory ad, category-education page, carousel.

**Failure modes.** Same hierarchy-of-effects critique as AIDA (below); comprehension-led copy
skews rational and under-performs in entertainment-mode feeds; DAGMAR was historically
criticised for over-weighting communication metrics against sales.

**Length/format.** Comprehension needs room: weak at 125 chars, strong at 30–60s video, strong
on landing pages. **A carousel is arguably its ideal Meta format — one card per stage.**

**Classification: STRUCTURE + DIAGNOSTIC.** Structure at the piece level; diagnostic at the
campaign level — DAGMAR's premise is diagnose-the-stuck-stage-first, then buy that stage.

### 1.4 Hook–Story–Offer — Russell Brunson (a diagnostic wearing a structure's clothes)

**Tier A for the definition; weak for efficacy.** Brunson's own wording
([Marketing Secrets podcast](https://marketingsecrets.libsyn.com/hook-story-offer-and-the-attractive-character)):

- **Hook** — "anything that grabs your attention." Explicitly **not just a headline**: headline,
  image, thumbnail, energy, personality, background.
- **Story** — "the goal of the story is to increase the perceived value of whatever it is
  you're going to be selling." A **price-anchor** function, not an entertainment function.
- **Offer** — the product/CTA, presented once attention and perceived value exist.

His repeated diagnostic claim: **"if there's an issue in your funnel, it's always related to
either the hook, the story, or the offer."** He maps his own books onto the three
(*Traffic Secrets* = hooks, *Expert Secrets* = story, *DotCom Secrets* = funnel/offer).

**His own paid-social prescription** is explicit and directly relevant: treat Facebook and
Instagram as **hook-testing grounds** — post something, test a hook, notice what stops *your
own* scroll, replicate the mechanic. Operationally: **hold story and offer constant, iterate
hooks in volume.**

**Failure modes, plainly.** Provenance is info-marketing, not tested direct response; the
ClickFunnels ecosystem draws substantive criticism for upsell architecture and income-claim
promoter culture ([ScamRisk](https://www.scamrisk.com/clickfunnels-review-is-russell-brunson-legit/),
[BehindMLM](https://behindmlm.com/mlm-reviews/click-funnels-review-internet-sales-funnel-2-levels-of-recruitment/)) —
**no FTC action was found; do not claim one.** No controlled test or published split-test
corpus exists. It is **nearly unfalsifiable** ("it's always one of three") — a feature for a
triage taxonomy, a bug as an efficacy claim. And it under-specifies: it names three parts and
never says how to write any of them.

**Length/format.** **The best Meta fit in the field.** Hook = first line of primary text +
first 1–2 seconds of video + thumbnail, which maps onto the 125-char truncation point almost
exactly. Scales identically at 15s / 30s / 60s.

**Classification: DIAGNOSTIC first, thin STRUCTURE second.** Its honest use is failure
attribution + a fan-out axis (N hooks × 1 story × 1 offer).

**Live tension to note:** Brunson's Hook (maximise stopping) directly opposes Fortin's Qualify
(filter the wrong prospect out) — §5.2. On machine-optimised paid traffic that is a
consequential doctrine choice, not a stylistic one.

### 1.5 Motion's concept→iteration model + the three metric triads

**Tier B (practitioner, high trust).** [Motion glossary](https://motionapp.com/library/glossary/creative-concept-vs-variant),
[Motion iterations guide](https://motionapp.com/blog/creative-iterations-for-winning-ads),
[Motion creative strategy](https://motionapp.com/blog/creative-strategy).

- **Concept** = "the big structural idea; before/after transformation, customer testimonial,
  founder rant, product demo."
- **Variant/iteration** = "a small change within that concept; different hook, different color
  palette, different opening clip." Tracked v1/v2/v3.
- **Angle** = "the perspective you take to present your product."
- **Hook** = "the initial attention-grabbers… designed to capture your customer's attention
  quickly."

**The genuinely useful diagnostic part — three metric pairings, each localising the failure to
a different part of the ad:**
1. **Thumbstop vs CTR** — does it stop the scroll *and* earn the click?
2. **CTR vs video completion** — do they watch fully yet not click?
3. **CTR vs conversion rate** — do clicks convert?

Motion's own walk-back is worth recording: "Meta's Andromeda algorithm has **reduced the
effectiveness of iterations**, though some value remains."

**Failure modes.** Combinatorial explosion produces near-duplicates that Meta explicitly does
*not* count as diversification (§6.2); the triads need per-creative volume small accounts
lack; the model has **no opinion at all on what the concept should be** — it starts after
ideation.

**Classification: STRUCTURE + DIAGNOSTIC.** Not a generator.

## 2. Problem-led flows

### 2.1 PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

**Tier C for origin.** No clean originator. Most-repeated attribution is **Gary Bencivenga**;
Copyhackers instead quotes **Dan Kennedy** calling it "the most reliable copywriting formula
for sales ever invented" ([Copyhackers formula guide](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/)).
Kennedy's *The Ultimate Sales Letter* carries an elaborated variant. **Neither is a documented
act of coinage — treat PAS as direct-response folk canon, not an authored framework.**

**Claims.** Name a felt problem → amplify its cost/consequence → resolve it with the product.
**Inputs.** One named pain; its felt cost; one mechanism that removes it.
**Output.** A short-to-medium linear body: ad primary text, email, page section.

**Failure modes.** *Agitation overrun* is the standard critique — Copyhackers has a whole piece
on agitation done badly ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/write-compelling-agitation-copy/)).
Practitioner criticism converges on: exaggerating or inventing problems, false urgency,
fear-mongering, and solutions that don't actually solve
([CrazyEgg](https://www.crazyegg.com/blog/pas-framework/)). Second: PAS is the most-copied
structure in existence, so it reads generic at scale — **directly relevant to the map's
"same-shaped copy" driver.**

**Length/format — and the 125-char question.** See §7.4. Short answer: PAS's short-form fit is
**real but not caused by** the 125-char fold; the fold amplifies an advantage PAS already has
by virtue of having only three beats.

**Classification: STRUCTURE.** It tells you beat order and nothing about which problem to pick.

---

### 2.2 PASO — …+ Outcome

**Tier B/C.** The four-letter form traces most concretely to **Belinda Weaver
(Copywrite Matters)**, who presents it explicitly as *her extension* of PAS: "Turn PAS into
PASO by **adding an outcome** to your reader journey"
([Copywrite Matters](https://www.copywritematters.com/paso-copywriting-formula/)). She does not
claim to have invented PAS.

**Claim.** PAS ends on the product, leaving the reader imagining a purchase rather than a life;
**O** re-points the ending at the reader's changed situation.
**Extra input.** One specific, preferably measurable/time-bound after-state.
**Failure mode.** "Outcome" collapses into a restated benefit unless it is specific — and in a
diet category the specific, time-bound outcome is exactly the regulated claim (§6.5).

**Length/format.** **The best fit in this list for a ~125-char primary text** — four beats
compress to four clauses. Also maps cleanly onto 15–30s video (problem ≤2s, agitate to ~8s,
solution, outcome + CTA).

**Classification: STRUCTURE.**

**Do not confuse with PASOP.** Copyhackers' **PASOP** = Problem, Agitation, Solution, Outcome,
**New Problem** — and it is **not a single-piece structure at all**: it is an *email-sequence*
device, each email ending on a newly opened problem the next email answers, across a ~6-email
/ 14-day drip ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/),
[Copyhackers email guide](https://copyhackers.com/2022/02/definitive-email-guide/)). The
terminal P is "new Problem", **not "Proof"** — a frequent third-party mis-gloss. Classify it as
STRUCTURE at the *sequence* level; it is not an ad shape.

### 2.3 PASTOR — Ray Edwards

**Tier A — the cleanest attribution in the whole sweep.** Edwards' own wording, from his own
podcast episode #031 ([rayedwards.com/go/031](https://www.rayedwards.com/go/031)) and his
P.A.S.T.O.R. Framework® page ([rayedwards.com/go/pastor](https://www.rayedwards.com/go/pastor)):

- **P — Problem**
- **A — Amplify**
- **S — Story *and* Solution**
- **T — Transformation *and* Testimony**
- **O — Offer**
- **R — Response**

Framing is the shepherd metaphor: the copywriter *pastors* the reader, "loving, caring, and
protective toward your readers and customers."

**The letters have drifted in third-party retellings** — P as "Person/Problem/Pain", S as
"Story/Solution/**System**", T with Testimony/Transformation reversed, and flat summaries that
render it "Problem, Amplify, Solution, Transformation, Offer, Response", **dropping Story and
Testimony entirely** ([The Marketing Onion](https://www.themarketingonion.com/pastor-copywriting-framework/),
[Cooler Insights](https://coolerinsights.com/2017/03/copywriting-tips-pastor/)). Ray Edwards
*also* has a distinct, shorter **4 Ps — Problem, Promise, Proof, Proposal** — catalogued by
Copyhackers, which is the ad-length member of his family and is **a different thing from the
Picture/Promise/Prove/Push 4 Ps** in §5.1.

**Doctrine implication:** if PASTOR is named, pin it to Edwards' six and record that **S and T
are each two-part beats**. A skill writing "S = Solution" has silently deleted the story
requirement, which is most of the framework's value.

**Inputs.** Problem; amplification material; one narrative; **at least one real testimonial**
plus a described before→after transformation; a fully specified offer; one explicit ask.
**Output.** Long-form sales page, VSL, or webinar pitch.

**Failure modes.** Testimony is load-bearing and is exactly what a Vietnamese
meal-replacement brand can least freely use — weight-loss testimonials and before/after
transformation claims are among the most-restricted creative categories on Meta (§6.5), and
Vietnamese health-food advertising rules govern testimonial-as-proof-of-efficacy.
**PASTOR without T is not PASTOR.** Second: it is a sales-page formula routinely retrofitted to
social, producing bloated ads.

**Length/format.** **Poor for a Meta primary text.** Home: landing page, VSL, long email,
webinar. **Classification: STRUCTURE + embedded proof checklist.**

### 2.4 The Five Objections — no time / no money / won't work for me / don't believe you / don't need it

**Tier D. This is a finding.** There is **no canonical originator.** All the usual candidates
were checked and none holds:

- **Masterson / AWAI** — AWAI's canonical checklists are the 4 U's and the peer-review system;
  **no AWAI page presents a "five basic objections" framework.**
- **Joanna Wiebe / Copyhackers** — works objections hard, but through *different* devices: the
  "even if" objection-stomper and the objection-busting offer
  ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/3-essential-copy-techniques/)). Their 200+-formula
  catalogue does not contain it.
- **Jay Abraham / Bob Bly** — no primary page found asserting authorship.
- Pages that do present it are aggregators
  ([Swipefile](https://swipefile.com/the-5-basic-objections-copywriting-formula),
  [Focus Copy](https://focuscopy.com/the-key-to-handling-objections-in-copy/)) — and several
  restate it with **entirely different labels** (Widenhouse's is *Overwhelm / Value /
  Skepticism / Fear / Complacency*, [nonprofitcopywriter](http://www.nonprofitcopywriter.com/basic-objections.html)).

Verdict: **useful list, no verified originator, no canonical wording — even the count and
labels drift.** Cite as "the standard five-objection checklist (unattributed direct-response
folk canon)". Nearest *sourced* cousins: Schwartz's awareness levels and Fortin's Transition
beat.

**Inputs.** The live objection set for the persona — ideally harvested from real
comments/DMs/consultations, not assumed.
**Output.** Either a pass/fail audit over finished copy, **or one angle per objection — five
ads from one product.**

**Failure modes.** (i) **As a checklist over a short ad it is destructive** — you cannot answer
five objections in 125 characters, and trying produces defensive, hedged copy. (ii) **Raising
an objection can install it** — naming "no money" to a cold audience introduces a price concern
they didn't have. (iii) The five are not equally weighted per market: for a Vietnamese diet
product the dominant real objections are typically *"it won't work for me / I've failed
before"* and *"is this safe / is this genuine Cambridge"* — **the latter is not in the
canonical five at all**, which argues for deriving the objection set empirically.

**Length/format.** As a checklist: long-form only. **As a generator: ideal for paid social —
one objection per ad, one objection per 125-char primary text.**

**Classification: GENERATOR primarily, DIAGNOSTIC secondarily (funnel-level: "which objection
is our drop-off?"), and a bad STRUCTURE.** It is one of only two items in this sweep that
cleanly fans a single product into multiple distinct ad angles.

## 3. Attention-led flows

### 3.1 AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

**Tier B, with a contested origin.** Conventional attribution is **Elias St. Elmo Lewis, 1898**
([Oxford Reference](https://www.oxfordreference.com/display/10.1093/oi/authority.20110803095432783),
[ProvenModels](https://www.provenmodels.com/547/aidasales-funnel/elias-st.-elmo-lewis)). **A
2023 paper by Akinori Iwamoto challenges this**, arguing the dominant Lewis hypothesis is
"unsupported or based on weak evidence" and crediting **Frank H. Dukesmith** and **Arthur
Frederick Sheldon** instead
([Carleton OJS](https://ojs.library.carleton.ca/index.php/pcharm/article/view/4340/3310) —
PDF fetched as binary; claim taken from the indexed abstract/summary, flag as
secondary-verified). Corroborating: **Dukesmith (1904)** published attention/interest/desire/
**conviction**; the AIDA acronym itself first appears in **C. P. Russell (1921)**; **Sheldon
(*Successful Selling*, 1911)** popularised it and added *permanent satisfaction* (AIDAS)
([MindTools](https://www.mindtools.com/a7ajkec/aida-attention-interest-desire-action/),
[hierarchy-of-effects survey PDF](https://repository.bakrie.ac.id/162/1/KOM-Artikel-009%20Hierarchy%20of%20effects%20model%20in%20advertising%20with%20cover.pdf)).

**Criticism is the strongest of any item here, and it is academic, not practitioner.** The
model "originated as a quick mnemonic… not backed by strong scientific research, although it
was later taken both literally and seriously by academic researchers." Vakratsas & Ambler's
extensive review of advertising-effects literature found **little empirical support for
hierarchical models**; the family also omits all post-purchase effects
([Wikipedia/AISDALSLove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AISDALSLove),
[MSI, "Is There a Hierarchy of Effects in Advertising?"](https://thearf-org-unified-admin.s3.amazonaws.com/MSI/2020/12/MSI_Report_20-139.pdf),
[Emerald](https://www.emerald.com/xjm/article/19/1/59/389315/Evolutionary-framework-of-hierarchy-of-effects)).

**Length/format.** Four beats do not fit 125 chars comfortably; in practice A+I land in the ad
and D+A on the destination. **Classification: STRUCTURE.**

### 3.2 AIDCA / IDCA

**AIDCA** inserts **Conviction** — a pre-close proof beat — between Desire and Action.
Copyhackers: "AIDCA simply adds in a pre-close moment of 'conviction'"
([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/)). Historically this is a
**restoration**, not an extension: Dukesmith's 1904 four steps *were* attention/interest/
desire/conviction. No individual invented AIDCA.

**Conviction demands** third-party evidence, credentials, demonstration/mechanism, guarantee /
risk reversal, testimonials, numbers — it answers "why should I believe this is true?" as
distinct from D's "do I want it?"

**IDCA** (the same minus Attention) is catalogued by Copyhackers explicitly for **traffic that
has already been stopped by an ad** — i.e. the landing page a Meta ad points at. **Useful
pairing: ad = A, landing page = IDCA.**

**Diet-category failure mode.** C is where a regulated brand hits a wall: the proof it wants
(weight-loss testimonials, before/after) is the proof Meta and Vietnamese food-advertising
rules most restrict. Conviction has to be carried by **institutional** proof (clinical
heritage, medical supervision, licensing) rather than **outcome** proof.

**Classification: STRUCTURE + embedded proof checklist.**

### 3.3 ACCA — see §1.3 (it is a router, not just a skeleton).

### 3.4 SLAP — Stop, Look, Act, Purchase

**Tier D. Recommend excluding — and here is why, on two independent grounds.**

**Provenance.** No originator exists in any traceable source. The most credible page carrying
it is Copyhackers' catalogue, which lists it with the note that it suits products
"relatively inexpensive to sell" and **gives no attribution**
([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/)). Every other source is
an aggregator, an SEO listicle, an infographic, or AI-generated content. Several recent ones
have retro-fitted SLAP onto "scroll-stopping" language that did not exist when the acronym
would have been coined — a tell that the current gloss is written backwards from the letters.

**Structural.** Its four steps are **not four writing beats, they are four funnel events** —
stopping, reading, clicking and buying are things the *reader does*, not sections you *write*.
Compare PASO, where each letter names a paragraph. SLAP instructs almost nothing. Its content
is already covered by AIDA (same funnel events, far better documented); it is effectively an
undocumented AIDA re-skin.

**Domain mismatch.** Its stated domain is low-consideration, low-price impulse purchase. A
Cambridge-style meal-replacement programme is high-consideration, high-price, health-adjacent,
with a consultation step — the opposite.

**Classification: not a framework. A funnel-stage vocabulary wearing a structure's clothes.**

## 4. Transformation / story flows

### 4.1 BAB — Before, After, Bridge

**Tier C/D for origin.** Widely attributed to **Bob Bly**, but only on aggregator pages; **no
primary Bly source was found stating it.** Popularised in social-media marketing by
[Buffer's formula post](https://buffer.com/resources/copywriting-formulas/). Treat the Bly
attribution as unverified.

**Claim.** Current painful state → the state once the problem is gone → the product as the
bridge between them.
**Inputs.** A vivid felt "before"; a desirable "after"; a credible mechanism connecting them.
**Output.** Short copy — landing page, case study, email, ad.

**Failure modes.** (i) **The shame failure** — BAB "is used in ways that shame people into
buying, by highlighting their struggles as personal failures, exaggerating pain points, or
promising unrealistic transformations" ([Meg Brunson](https://megbrunson.com/before-after-bridge/)).
For a diet brand this is the framework's *default* failure, not an edge case. (ii) Three beats
are too basic for complex or sophisticated audiences. (iii) It works when the product
*generates a positive outcome*, and poorly when it *avoids a negative one*. (iv) In a diet
context BAB's natural output **is the before/after transformation asset** that Meta restricts
(§6.5).

**Length/format.** Fits 125 chars (three beats); strongest in video where the "before" can be
shown rather than described.
**Classification: STRUCTURE**, with a weak GENERATOR edge (different "afters" = different ads).

### 4.2 Star–Story–Solution

**Tier C. The Robert Collier attribution does not hold up.** Collier's *Letter Book* is
genuinely foundational for story-led sales letters and is the source of "always enter the
conversation already taking place in the customer's mind"
([ActiveCampaign's Collier quote collection](https://www.activecampaign.com/blog/robert-collier-quotes),
[Jeremy Mac on the Letter Book](https://www.jeremymac.com/blogs/news/7-copywriting-tips-from-the-robert-collier-letter-book-that-still-work-today)) —
but **no source was found in which Collier names or defines "Star-Story-Solution."** Thematic
ancestry, not authorship. Catalogued by
[Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/).

**Claim.** Introduce the Star (customer, founder, or product personified) → narrate what
happened → reveal the Solution. A reader who identifies with a Star lowers skepticism before
the pitch arrives, so proof lands as narrative rather than assertion.
**Inputs.** A **real, specific, named** person; a genuine arc with a turn; the product as agent
of the turn. Copyhackers' note: the reader must be able to occupy the Star.

**Failure modes.** Requires a real story — invented Stars are detectable and, in health
advertising, actionable. In a diet context it collapses into exactly the **weight-loss
transformation testimonial** that Meta restricts: the framework's natural output is the
brand's highest-compliance-risk asset. Story delays the offer, so on cold traffic the Story
beat is where drop-off concentrates.

**Length/format.** Poor at 125 chars; **excellent at 30–60s+ video**, where the Star can be
*shown*, compressing setup that text needs paragraphs for. Strong for creator/UGC.
**Classification: STRUCTURE + GENERATOR** — "pick a different Star" is a legitimate fan-out
axis (one product, N customers = N distinct ads).

### 4.3 Hook–Story–Offer — see §1.4 (classified as a diagnostic).

## 5. Proof / persuasion flows

### 5.1 The 4 Ps — Picture, Promise, Prove, Push

**Tier B/C.** Attributed to direct-mail pioneer **Henry Hoke Sr.**, 1930s
([copywriting.school](https://copywriting.school/formulas/pppp-picture-promise-prove-push)).
Attribution is consistent across sources but no primary Hoke document was reachable.

**Claim.** Paint a vivid word-picture the reader can step into → state the promise that ends
that scene or delivers it → prove it → push for immediate action. **Its defining feature is
order: the picture comes BEFORE the promise**, so desire is built before the claim is made.

**Watch the name collision.** Three distinct "4 Ps" circulate:
- **Picture / Promise / Prove / Push** (Hoke) — the one the ticket means.
- **Promise / Picture / Proof / Push** — a reordered variant common in cold-email advice
  ([GMass](https://www.gmass.co/blog/promise-picture-proof-push/)); claim-first, and therefore
  a genuinely different piece of doctrine.
- **Problem / Promise / Proof / Proposal** — **Ray Edwards'**, which Copyhackers explicitly
  *prefers* over the "Picture" variant for homepages and lead-gen pages "to avoid seeming
  scammy" ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/)).

Any doctrine naming "4 Ps" must say which. **Sources disagree, and the disagreement is
substantive (picture-first vs claim-first).**

**Inputs.** A concrete scene; one promise; a proof asset; an action.
**Length/format.** Four beats fit a 125-char primary text tightly, and fit 15–30s video well.
**Failure mode.** The Picture beat in a weight-loss context is where idealised-body imagery
and second-person body assertions creep in (§6.5).
**Classification: STRUCTURE.**

### 5.2 QUEST — Qualify, Understand, Educate, Stimulate, Transition

**Tier A. Michel Fortin is the originator — confirmed.** His own site:
"I developed the QUEST formula over decades…"
([michelfortin.com/copywriting/quest/](https://michelfortin.com/copywriting/quest/),
[michelfortin.com/quest-formula/](https://michelfortin.com/quest-formula/)). Independent
catalogues concur ([Copyhackers](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/)).
Evidence-hygiene note: the current page is a rewritten, B2B-flavoured version framed around
"revenue leaks"; the 2009-era statements are the ones the copywriting community absorbed
([2009 repost](https://flylondonwomensaida.wordpress.com/2009/03/29/michel-fortins-copywriting-formula/)).

- **Qualify** — make "the right prospect feel seen fast," while **filtering out wrong-fit
  prospects**. Fortin: "Strong qualification also reinforces the prospect's identity as a
  buyer" and "if someone has to work to figure out whether your message applies to them, most
  won't bother." He notes it is "almost always underinvested."
- **Understand** — credible empathy; hidden costs and the risk of inaction.
- **Educate** — the solution *without pricing or the ask*: methodology, case studies, proof of
  concept.
- **Stimulate** — expanded benefits, social proof, the offer, urgency, risk reversal.
- **Transition** — the next step + final objection handling.

**Why Qualify matters specifically on Meta.** You are paying per impression against a
machine-optimised audience. A qualifying first line is the only lever *inside the creative*
that shapes who self-selects into the click. On broad targeting, an unqualified hook buys
cheap clicks from people who will never buy, **which then teaches the optimiser to find more
of them.** QUEST is the one framework whose first beat is about traffic *quality* rather than
volume — the direct opposite of Brunson's Hook. For a consultation-gated, high-ticket
programme, cheap unqualified clicks are the expensive failure mode. Qualify is also a
compliance asset: naming the intended user is safer than a universal efficacy claim.

**Failure modes.** Qualifying shrinks reach, which can raise CPM and slow learning-phase exit —
a deliberate tradeoff, not an accident; over-qualifying reads as exclusionary; it is a
sales-page formula routinely misapplied to ads.

**Length/format.** **The Qualify beat alone fits a 125-char primary text and is arguably the
highest-value use of those characters.** The full five need a page or a 60s+ VSL. Natural
split: **Q as the ad's opening-line rule; U-E-S-T on the destination** (pairs with IDCA).

**Classification: STRUCTURE + DIAGNOSTIC.** Q is an audience-fit test applied *before* writing;
T is an objection audit applied *after*.

### 5.3 RMBC — Research, Mechanism, Brief, Copy — Stefan Georgi

**Tier A.** [stefanpaulgeorgi.com](https://www.stefanpaulgeorgi.com/the-rmbc-method-for-better-copy/),
[rmbcmethod.com](https://www.rmbcmethod.com/).

- **Research** — "where all of the magic happens": surprising insights, the exact language the
  audience uses, pain points, and **what they have already tried and failed with** — from
  forums, reviews, direct investigation.
- **Mechanism** — "a two-part logical connection", and Georgi is emphatic these are **two
  distinct things**:
  - **Unique Mechanism Behind the Problem** — the real, previously-unnamed *cause* of their
    failure;
  - **Unique Mechanism Behind the Solution** — why *this* works where everything else failed.
- **Brief** — research + mechanism turned into answerable questions, whose answers are written
  as **pre-drafted copy chunks**.
- **Copy** — execution against a seven-section outline: Lead, Background Story, Problem
  Mechanism, Solution Mechanism, Product Reveal, Close, FAQ.

**It is an intake/production PROCESS, not a copy shape — confirmed, with one qualification.**
Three of its four steps happen before any copy exists; its stated purpose is throughput
(Georgi built it to "pump out 12 sales letters per month"). The C step *does* carry a
structure, so RMBC is a process that **terminates in** a structure — but the structure is its
least distinctive part.

**Relationship to Schwartz stage 3 — stated properly.** Schwartz says *use a mechanism when the
market has reached stage 3*; Georgi says *find the mechanism, always*. Georgi's addition beyond
Schwartz is the **problem/solution split**: Schwartz's mechanism is a mechanism *of the
solution*; Georgi front-loads a mechanism *of the problem*, which **absolves the prospect of
blame for prior failure** before the solution arrives.

**Why this is load-bearing for this brand.** The Vietnamese weight-loss market is plainly at
**sophistication stage 4+** — the audience has seen trà giảm cân, detox, thuốc giảm cân, keto,
IF and countless MLM shakes, and has usually failed at several. A stage-1/2 claim ("giảm cân
hiệu quả") is dead on arrival. A **problem-mechanism** — "here is why every previous attempt
failed, and it wasn't your fault" — converts the audience's failure history from an objection
into the proof of your premise. And unlike a benefit claim, a *mechanism* explanation is
comparatively defensible under Meta's health rules and Vietnamese food-advertising
restrictions, because it explains *how the product works* rather than *what results you get*.

**Inputs.** Substantial genuine voice-of-customer material. **The heaviest input requirement in
the field**, and a real dependency: RMBC without real VOC degenerates into invented mechanism.
**Output.** A research brief plus a long-form sales letter or VSL. **Not an ad.**

**Failure modes.** (i) **Fabricated mechanism** — the method's commercial pressure is to
*always have* a unique mechanism, which in supplement/diet copy has repeatedly produced
invented pseudo-science. In a health category with a Vietnamese regulator that is not a
stylistic risk, it is a legal one. **Any RMBC-derived mechanism here must be traceable to the
brand's actual clinical/KB documentation.** (ii) Provenance is the info-product/supplement
direct-response world; the accessible detail sits behind paid courses or pirated re-uploads —
treat those as low-trust. (iii) Expensive: three steps before a word of copy. (iv) No
independent efficacy evidence.

**Length/format.** Produces no ad-length artifact of its own — **but its Mechanism output is the
best raw material for ads in the field**: a problem-mechanism compresses to a single 125-char
line and a 3-second video hook better than almost anything else.

**Classification: GENERATOR + PROCESS**, with a minor STRUCTURE tail. **It is the only item in
this sweep that is genuinely about finding something to say and that ships a defined intake
stage.** Nothing else competes for the research-layer slot.

### 5.4 The 12-step VSL — and the attribution error in it

**The "12-step VSL" is DigitalMarketer's, not Jim Edwards'.** DigitalMarketer's 12-Step Video
Sales Letter Script Builder:

1. Attention-Getting Open · 2. Identify the Problem · 3. Aggravate the Problem · 4. Present the
Solution · 5. Establish Authority · 6. Explain How it Works · 7. Offer (CTA #1 — **Gain**) ·
8. Guarantee · 9. Offer (CTA #2 — **Logic**) · 10. Warning · 11. Offer (CTA #3 — **Fear**) ·
12. Social Proof (optional)
([DigitalMarketer script builder](https://www.digitalmarketer.com.cach3.com/lp/12-step-vsl-script-builder/special.php.html),
[current workshop page](https://www.digitalmarketer.com/workshop/craft-a-high-converting-vsl/)).
Its defining feature is the **triple close** — the offer made three times on three registers
(gain → logic → fear), separated by a guarantee and a warning.

**Jim Edwards' own formula is TEN steps** ([thejimedwardsmethod.com](https://thejimedwardsmethod.com/the-great-video-sales-letter-script-formula/)):
Open With a Shocking Statement · State the Problem and WHY It's a Big Deal · Agitate the
Problem ("rub their noses in WHY this is a lot worse than they think") · Make It Even Worse
("Hit them – HARD – on an emotional level") · Introduce The Solution · Credibility · Proof ·
Exactly What You Get · Specific Reasons To Act Now · The Close. He **also** publishes a
separate, much shorter **PAS-based VSL template**
([PDF](https://downloads.thejimedwardsmethod.com/pdf/landing-page-secrets/Video-Sales-Letter-Template-PAS.pdf)) —
so **Edwards himself has no single canonical VSL structure.**

Third-party versions compress further (a 7-step process at
[Mastropierro](https://carminemastropierro.com/how-to-write-a-vsl-script/); Copy Posse has its
own variant). **Harmon Brothers are a different tradition, not a competing VSL version** —
Squatty Potty / Purple / Poo-Pourri; their artifact is a 14-Day Video Script Challenge for
short, comedic, shareable ads ([Harmon Brothers University](https://harmonbrothersuniversity.com/)).
The operative contrast: **VSLs run 5–30 minutes; short video scripts run 30 seconds+**.
**Todd Brown** sits on the DR side (big-idea-led) but **no primary Todd Brown page defining a
numbered VSL step-list was found** — do not cite a "Todd Brown VSL structure" with a step count.

**Failure modes.** (i) Steps 3+4 are the **aggravation double-tap** — "rub their noses in it",
"hit them HARD on an emotional level" — the single most dangerous instruction in this sweep for
a diet brand: escalating emotional pain about a viewer's body is both a Meta policy risk
(personal-attributes / body-image) and an ethical problem in an eating-adjacent category. If
this framework enters the pipeline, steps 3–4 need an explicit written constraint. (ii) The
12-step's **fear-based third CTA (step 11)** compounds it. (iii) **The step count is unstable
across every source** (7 / 10 / 12) — "follow the VSL formula" is not a reproducible
instruction unless the version is pinned. (iv) Long VSLs have poor completion in social feeds;
they belong post-click.

**Length/format.** **The furthest from a Meta ad of anything in this sweep.** A 15–30s Meta
video can carry at most steps 1–2 plus a CTA — i.e. a hook and a problem, for which PASO or
Hook-Story-Offer are better tools. If a *short* video framework is wanted, the Harmon Brothers
tradition is the correct reference class, not the VSL tradition.

**Classification: STRUCTURE, purely and at maximum resolution.** Zero diagnostic content, zero
generative content, minimum adaptability.

## 6. Feature translation, filters, and the platform layer

### 6.1 FAB / FABV

**FAB — Tier B/C.** Feature (what it is) → Advantage (what the feature *does*) → Benefit (what
that means *for this reader*). Genuinely canonical in **sales training**, not copywriting;
"originating from the world of direct sales and later popularized in modern marketing"
([Agile Brand Guide](https://agilebrandguide.com/wiki/marketing-funnel/features-advantages-benefits-fab/),
[LaunchNotes](https://www.launchnotes.com/glossary/features-advantages-and-benefits-analysis-fab-analysis-in-product-management-and-operations)).
**No individual originator is identifiable.** Copyhackers catalogues it as a **body-copy**
formula — explicitly *not* a whole-piece structure — and notes it works best "when prospects
have high awareness."

**FABV — Tier C, and the V is "Value", not "Verification".** The sourced expansion is
Features, Advantages, Benefits, **Value**
([Valens Research](https://www.valens-research.com/dynamic-marketing-communique/the-must-know-copywriting-formula-to-increase-readership-is-just-one-fab-click-away-tuesdays-write-with-the-pen-of-the-masters/)).
**No source expanding V as "Verification" was found.** If a proof beat is wanted, that is
AIDCA's C or PASTOR's T — FABV is not the framework to get it from. V is barely distinct from
B in practice (aggregate worth vs price, i.e. B restated at a higher altitude). **FAB is sound;
FABV is thin.**

**Inputs.** A complete, accurate feature inventory — **and the persona**, because the same
Advantage produces different Benefits for different people. FAB without a persona produces
generic benefits.
**Output.** Bullets, product-description sections, a body-copy block, comparison tables. Not a
whole ad.

**Failure modes.** The classic one is **stopping at A** and calling the functional consequence
a benefit. It is inherently **product-centric** — it starts from what you have, not what they
want, so it produces competent, undifferentiated copy and rarely a big idea (again: relevant
to the "bland, same-shaped" driver). In a diet category the **Benefit column is exactly where
efficacy claims get written**, and that column is regulated; features and advantages are safe.
FAB output is boring at top of funnel and useful at bottom.

**Length/format.** **One F→A→B chain fits a 125-char primary text comfortably** — possibly the
tightest single-idea unit available. A full FAB inventory does not: page, or carousel with one
feature per card.

**Classification: GENERATOR + local STRUCTURE.** Generator because it mechanically converts a
feature list into an angle list (N features × 1 persona = N candidate ads). Structure only at
the paragraph/bullet level. **It is not a piece-level structure.**

### 6.2 The 4 C's — Clear, Concise, Compelling, Credible

**Tier B.** Attributed to **Bob Bly** (*Business-to-Business Marketing Handbook*, reproduced in
*The Copywriter's Handbook*) ([ContentBot](https://www.contentbot.ai/blog/news/the-four-cs-formula/),
[WordStream](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/ws/2020/04/16/copywriting-formulas)). **Honest
caveat: no page on bly.com stating the four terms in that exact list was found** — the
attribution is consistent and plausible but rests on the book, not a verifiable web primary.

**Filter, not sequence — confirmed.** Copyhackers files the 4 C's under **"pre-publishing
quality check"**, the same bucket as the 4 U's, and *not* among the sequential formulas. There
is no "start with Clear, then do Concise" reading anywhere. **You cannot write to the 4 C's;
you can only score against them.** ("Concise" means no word doing no work — *not* "short".)

**Input.** A finished draft — which is itself the proof that it's a filter.
**Output.** A pass/fail or scored judgement + a revision instruction. No new copy.

**Failure modes.** (i) **Entirely subjective, with no published rubric** — unlike the 4 U's, no
source gives a scale or a threshold. Operationalising it in an automated rating step means
**inventing** the scale, and the prose should say the scale is ours, not Bly's. (ii)
Clear/Concise/Credible are cheap to satisfy and **Compelling is not** — a draft can score 3/4
and still be dead, so an unweighted composite misleads. (iii) It is conservative by
construction: it can only reject, never generate, and applied early it will kill unusual angles
for being unclear when they are merely unfamiliar.

**Length/format.** **Format-agnostic** — identical at 125 chars, at 27 chars, at 30s, and on a
landing page. The only item with no length implication at all, which is what you'd expect of a
filter.

**Classification: DIAGNOSTIC (quality filter).** Sits *after* production, in the self-scoring
step. Natural division of labour: **4 U's rate the headline, 4 C's rate everything else.**

### 6.3 The 4 U's — Useful, Urgent, Unique, Ultra-specific

**Tier A/B. Michael Masterson via AWAI**, reproduced in Bly's *Copywriter's Handbook*
([AWAI's own review of the 4 U's](https://www.awai.com/2001/06/a-review-of-the-4-us/)).
Copyhackers files it as a **pre-publishing checklist**; Joanna Wiebe devoted an Unbounce
podcast episode to it ([Unbounce](https://unbounce.com/podcast/copywriting-formula-4-u/)).

**It ships an actual rubric — the only framework in this sweep that does.**
- Rate the headline **1 to 4** on each U.
- **Threshold: "if your headline doesn't rate a 3 or a 4 on at least three of the four U's,
  it's probably not as strong as it could be and you should consider doing a rewrite."**
- **Priority order when the U's conflict: Usefulness → Urgency → Uniqueness →
  Ultra-specificity.** It is a *priority ranking*, not four equal criteria — the detail most
  restatements drop and the operationally useful part.
- AWAI's **peer-review** variant: average the group's scores; **below 3.2** → facilitator asks
  for improvement suggestions; ~3.5+ is strong ([AWAI peer review](https://www.awai.com/2007/11/peer-review/)).

**Scope.** Headline-only is the right primary framing, but the AWAI primary source applies it
to **bullets** too, and notes "there is no one 'right answer'". Honest scope: **headlines,
subject lines, and bullets** — short high-attention units. It evaluates no structure, no
argument, no offer.

**Failure modes.** (i) **Urgency and Ultra-specificity are the compliance pressure points for a
diet brand** — "giảm 5kg trong 14 ngày" scores 4/4 on both and is precisely the claim Meta's
health policy and Vietnamese food-advertising rules restrict. **A pipeline that optimises
headlines for the 4 U's without a compliance gate will systematically manufacture
non-compliant headlines.** This is the most concrete doctrine risk in this document. (ii)
Urgency chased for its own sake produces fake scarcity. (iii) It rates the *line*, blind to
awareness/sophistication fit — a 4/4/4/4 headline aimed at the wrong stage still fails, which
is why it needs Schwartz beside it. (iv) Self-scoring drifts optimistic; AWAI's own answer is
a **second rater**.

**Length/format.** Purpose-built for exactly the units Meta gives you: the **27-char Feed
headline**, the 40-char max headline, the first line of primary text, video overlay text.

**Classification: DIAGNOSTIC (scored quality filter).** The most directly implementable as an
automated self-scoring step, and the one whose thresholds can be adopted verbatim rather than
invented.

### 6.4 The generators the canon is thin on

Three genuine generators from modern practice, none of them named "frameworks" in the classic
canon:

**Voice-of-customer / review mining — GENERATOR, unambiguously.** Standard loop:
**Extract → Categorize → Translate → Prioritize**
([Invesp](https://www.invespcro.com/blog/customer-review-mining/),
[DTCskills Review Mining Playbook](https://dtcskills.com/skills/review-mining-playbook)).
Output is *literal customer phrasing* used as hook/headline copy. Competitor reviews are mined
for what buyers dislike about alternatives → differentiation angle. Failure modes: reviewers
are already customers (survivorship bias — they skew *product-aware*, so mined angles
under-serve unaware/problem-aware); **for a Vietnamese diet brand the VOC corpus will be
comments, Facebook-group posts, Zalo threads and consultation notes, not Amazon-style
reviews.**

**Competitor ad-library teardown — GENERATOR (+ weak diagnostic).** Pull 25–30 in-market ads →
collection → tag hook/offer/CTA → check the ad's **timeline for longevity** → draft the brief
from the pattern ([Foreplay](https://www.foreplay.co/),
[ad-library→brief workflow](https://adlibrary.com/posts/from-ad-library-research-to-creative-brief-in-60-minutes)).
Failure modes: longevity is a *proxy*, not evidence — brands run losers out of inertia;
teardown produces convergence on category clichés (the opposite of diversification); it tells
you what exists, never what's missing.

**Denney's messaging-angle taxonomy — GENERATOR + DIAGNOSTIC, by the author's own description**
([Motion library — Dara Denney](https://motionapp.com/library/expert/dara-denney/)). Four
buckets: **Human Desires** (romance, connection, tranquility, power) · **Demographics** ·
**Strategy Angles** (celebrity, negative marketing, taboo, personal history) · **User Journey**
(awareness stages). Her process: divergent → 5–10 concepts → convergent filter (audience fit,
brand alignment, platform context) → small-budget tests.

**Levinger's emotional-driver model — GENERATOR.** Collect experiential/psychographic data —
"psychological fears and emotions surrounding a core issue" — not demographics; **test
emotional drivers rather than offers or angles** ([sarahlevinger.co](https://sarahlevinger.co/),
[Foreplay interview](https://www.foreplay.co/post/harnessing-psychology-in-creative-strategy-sarah-levingers-insights-on-consumer-behavior)).
Caveats: the "95% of decisions are subconscious" stat she cites is a widely-contested
industry claim; her three-dimension matrix is proprietary and not fully published, so it is not
independently reproducible.

**Hook-type taxonomies — GENERATOR only, and pure folklore.** A ~19–40-item list circulates in
near-identical form across tools (*Aspirational, Authority, Bold Claim, Contrarian, Curiosity,
Demographic Callout, Direct Address, FOMO, How-To, Myth Busting, Price Anchor, Shocking
Statement, Social Proof, Storytelling, Urgency, Warning*…)
([40-hook library](https://brandedby.co/article/the-ultimate-ad-hook-library-40-proven-hooks-example-angles-by-industry)).
Agreement is high on the core six or seven and low beyond; no list has an authoring authority;
the categories are not mutually exclusive. **Should not be used as a diagnostic** — "which hook
type wins" is not a stable, transferable finding, because hook type is confounded with
everything else in the ad.

**Also in the generator slot from classic canon:** Ogilvy's "do your homework → find the big
idea" (§8.2) and Schwartz's mass-desire analysis (§1.1).

### 6.5 Meta's own doctrine and policy — the constraint layer

**Performance 5 — and the fact that Meta ships two different lists.** List A (Meta newsroom):
Account Simplification · Creators for Direct Response (internal study: **39% sales lift**) ·
Creative Diversification (**32% more efficient DR outcomes, 9% incremental reach**) ·
Conversions API Quality Check · Business Results Validation ("last click attribution
undervalues Facebook and Instagram by 47%")
([Meta newsroom](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/meta-performance-5-techniques)).
List B (Meta Blueprint + Meta's partner-podcast page): account simplification, **automation**,
creative diversification, data quality, results validation — and it renames #3 "Creative
**Differentiation**"
([Blueprint path](https://www.facebookblueprint.com/student/path/253157-performance-5),
[Meta podcast page](https://www.facebook.com/business/marketing-partners/business-innovation-technology-podcast/performance-five-framework)).
**Do not treat "the Performance 5" as a stable canonical five.** The three present in both —
account simplification, creative diversification, data/measurement quality — are the safe core.
It is an *account-hygiene* framework wearing a creative-strategy label. **Classification:
STRUCTURE, at the account level.**

**Creative diversification — the single most useful first-party page for this decision.**
[Demystifying Creative Diversification](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/demystifying-creative-diversification):
- Diversification = "**the creation of distinct campaign assets tailored to different personas
  or use cases**", which must be "**distinct in their visuals, messaging, and formats**."
- Explicitly contrasted with **creative iteration** (minor A/B refinements). Meta's own worked
  example: iteration = "**two ads with identical visuals, but different text CTAs**";
  diversification = a Reels ad for one persona vs a creator partnership ad for another.

> **Meta itself says hook-swapping is NOT diversification.** That is first-party cover for
> treating **persona × route** as the diversification axis and hook/CTA as the iteration axis.

**Why creative is the remaining lever — inference, not a Meta statement.** Meta's engineering
post on **Andromeda** (Dec 2024) says it uses "an efficient hierarchical index to scale up to a
**large volume of ads creatives**", in a context where "more than a million advertisers used our
generative AI tools to create **more than 15 million ads in a month**"
([Meta Engineering](https://engineering.fb.com/2024/12/02/production-engineering/meta-andromeda-advantage-automation-next-gen-personalized-ads-retrieval-engine/)).
Meta built retrieval infrastructure for creative-volume explosion and moved targeting into
automation. The conclusion "therefore creative is the lever" is **well-founded practitioner
interpretation, not a Meta claim.**

**Hook mechanics — first-party, thin, and format-level only.** From
[Meta's Reels ads page](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads/facebook-instagram-reels-ads):
9:16 with audio, key messages in the safe zone; **Reels default to sound-on**; ads meeting the
criteria saw "2X higher delivery to the Reels placement" and "34.5% lower cost per result
compared to image ads"; embrace authenticity/UGC; shorter outperforms longer; vertical-first.
Plus branding **within the first 3 seconds** [snippet-only].
Three corrections worth carrying:
- **"Design for sound-off" is dated doctrine.** Current Reels guidance is the opposite. Both
  eras' pages still exist in the help centre simultaneously.
- **The 20%-text rule is dead as a rule** (Meta, Sept 2020: it no longer penalises text-heavy
  images in auction/delivery, and the overlay checker is gone). Less text remains a
  *performance* recommendation, not a delivery penalty.
- **"Hook rate" is not a Meta metric.** Meta ships **3-second video plays** and ThruPlays;
  hook rate and thumbstop are practitioner-derived ratios with no published Meta benchmark.
**Classification: STRUCTURE only.** Meta supplies no hook taxonomy and no hook generator.

**Testing structure — first-party.** A/B Test tool: "divide your audience into random,
**non-overlapping** groups… identical on all aspects except **one distinct variable**", run
"at least 2 weeks or up to 30 days" [snippet-only]. Significance:
"**a 65 percent or higher confidence percentage represents a winning result**", with suggested
power of 80% [snippet-only]
([About confidence in your tests](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/239549606692303)).
Learning phase: **~50 optimisation events per ad set per 7 days**
([About the learning phase](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/112167992830700)) [snippet-only].

> **Flag loudly: 65% confidence is a very permissive bar** — a 35% chance the "winner" is noise,
> against the 95% convention. A Meta A/B "winner" is not established fact.
>
> **And the requirements are mutually incompatible on a small budget:** a brand that can afford
> one ad set at 50 events/week cannot afford to split it in two for a non-overlapping test.
> **Small accounts structurally cannot run Meta's prescribed test.** Also, "one variable" is in
> direct tension with Meta's own diversification doctrine (vary visual + message + format
> *simultaneously*).

**Creative fatigue — deliberately non-numeric.** Meta's remedies:
"**Highlight a different product benefit, show a new use case, offer a fresh perspective or
feature different people and situations**", plus "check your opportunity score recommendations"
([Solving creative fatigue](https://www.facebook.com/business/learn/video/creative-fatigue)).
**Meta prescribes no rotation schedule** — any "refresh every 7/14/21 days" rule is folklore.
Note that Meta's own fatigue remedy list is a **four-axis mini-generator**.

**Health / weight-loss policy — the hard constraint on this brand's creative.**

| Rule | Source | Effect on creative |

Meta's own worked examples under Personal Attributes are directly transferable:
❌ "**Do you have diabetes?**" / ❌ "**Depression getting you down? Get help now.**" vs

> **The operative rule: second-person problem statements are the violation; third-person /

>
> **This bans the opening move of most of the problem-led canon.** PAS's P, PASTOR's P,

> Demographic-Callout / Direct-Address hooks are, applied literally to weight in the second
> person, policy violations. They have to be routed through third-person or product-side
> framing. **RMBC's problem-mechanism is the one high-leverage beat that supplies persuasive
> force without requiring an outcome claim or a second-person body assertion.**

Adjacent: [Suicide, Self-Injury and Eating Disorders](https://transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/suicide-selfinjury-eating-disorders/)

## 7. The four claims the ticket asked to test

### 7.1 Claim: "the Schwartz grid is the real router; the rest are largely interchangeable once the reader is placed"

**Verdict: half-supported, and the supported half is narrower than the claim.**

**What the primary sources support.** Schwartz himself gives exactly one routing rule, and it
is about the *starting point*, not the *structure*: aware of the product → start with the
product; unaware → start with the desire. **Masterson & Forde extend that into the only
awareness→X mapping in the canon that has a primary source — and it maps awareness to a LEAD
TYPE (Offer / Promise / Problem-Solution / Big Secret / Proclamation / Story), not to a copy
formula** (§1.2). Sophistication then constrains the *claim type* independently
(plain / enlarged / mechanism / better mechanism / identity). Both axes are diagnostics that
constrain what is legal; neither supplies a skeleton. That is genuinely router-shaped.

**What is NOT supported: the specific mapping quoted in the ticket.** The canonical mapping the
ticket cites — *AIDA when problem-unaware, PAS when problem-aware/solution-unknown, FAB or BAB
at solution comparison, PASTOR when warm* — **appears only on C/D-tier sources.** It is not in
*Breakthrough Advertising*, not in *Great Leads*, not in Copyhackers' 200-formula catalogue,
not on Ray Edwards' or Michel Fortin's or Bob Bly's own sites. The pages that state it are SEO
copywriting blogs, e.g.
[ismelguerrero.com](https://ismelguerrero.com/blog/copywriting-frameworks-guide/) and
[editorialge](https://editorialge.com/copywriting-frameworks-for-marketers/), which give it as:
"problem-unaware readers need AIDA to educate first; problem-aware readers need PAS to amplify
urgency; solution-aware readers need FAB or BAB to differentiate; brand-aware readers need
PASTOR or PPPP to close." **Treat it as a plausible, unsourced synthesis — not canon.**

**Sources that disagree, in three different ways.**
1. **A different mapping, same tier.** A widely-shared framework-per-buyer-stage version reads
   "Awareness → AIDA. Interest → PAS. Decision → QUEST. Action → 4Ps. Retention → BAB"
   ([Threads/dailyemailmarketing](https://www.threads.com/@dailyemailmarketing/post/DUPcfEeEoss/)) —
   putting BAB at *retention*, not solution comparison, and QUEST at decision. Same evidence
   tier, incompatible answer.
2. **A different router axis entirely.** Rob Palmer (working copywriter): "the formula you
   choose depends on three factors: **Format, Audience temperature, and Offer complexity**"
   ([robpalmer.com](https://robpalmer.com/blog/copywriting-formulas)). Awareness is only one of
   three, and format is co-equal.
3. **Not interchangeable at all — they differ in what they demand and where they live.** This
   sweep found that the flows differ on axes the awareness grid does not touch:
   PASTOR/QUEST/VSL/RMBC are **long-form or pre-copy** artifacts that cannot be an ad; the
   4 U's and 4 C's are **filters** that cannot be written to; the Five Objections and FAB are
   **generators** whose checklist use at ad length is actively harmful; SLAP prescribes nothing;
   ACCA answers a *comprehension* barrier that awareness-stage routing does not model. **Once
   the reader is placed, the remaining choice is still constrained by format, by what proof
   assets exist, and by what the health-ads policy permits.**

**Note also that the awareness axis has lost its targeting counterpart.** Post-Advantage+ you
cannot buy an awareness stage — one ad set holds all five simultaneously. The grid is now a
**message lens and a portfolio-coverage audit**, not a campaign structure.

### 7.2 Claim: "layering beats picking" — one piece opening BAB, deepening with PAS, closing with AIDA

**Verdict: the general principle has decent practitioner support; the specific ordering does
not, and the sources that endorse layering give different orders.**

**Supported.** Rob Palmer, a working copywriter: "**Most high-converting copy blends elements
from multiple formulas**" — "a sales page might use AIDA for overall structure, PAS for the
opening, and the 4 Ps for individual sections" — and "formulas are tools, not rules"
([robpalmer.com](https://robpalmer.com/blog/copywriting-formulas)). Structural corroboration:
the long-form formulas in the canon **already are compositions of smaller units** — PASTOR
nests PAS in its P-A and Star-Story-Solution in its S; Perry Belcher's 21-part letter puts the
CTA at step 19; DigitalMarketer's 12-step nests PAS in steps 2–4 and repeats the offer three
times ([Copyhackers catalogue](https://copyhackers.com/2015/10/copywriting-formula/)). So
layering is descriptively how the long formulas are built.

**Not supported: the specific BAB → PAS → AIDA order.** The pages that recommend layering give
**different and mutually inconsistent orders** — "start with PAS, shift to BAB, finish with
AIDA"; "start with an AIDA hook, use PAS to build urgency, close with proof"; Palmer's "AIDA
for structure, PAS for the opening, 4 Ps for sections". **No source states the ticket's order.**
And all the layering-specific pages are C-tier.

**Caveat the decision session should weigh.** Layering is a **long-form** claim. Every cited
example is a sales page or a letter. At 125 characters there is room for one flow, not three —
so "layering beats picking" and "PAS fits short-form" are claims about different artifacts.
The honest reading is: **layer on the destination; pick on the ad.** Palmer's own selection
rule already says format is one of the three deciding factors.

### 7.3 Claim: "3–5 variations per ad set, each on a different framework or angle"

**Verdict: no first-party evidence for the number exists, and the closest thing Meta ever
published has been WITHDRAWN. The *shape* of the claim (several genuinely different variations
per set) is better supported than the *number*.**

**What Meta used to say.** Meta's help page *About managing ad volume* previously recommended
"**Use 6 or fewer creatives per ad set… Once you've added more than 6 ads, there is little
marginal benefit**" ([Meta help](https://business.facebook.com/business/help/2720085414702598);
page is client-rendered and could not be fetched — quote via
[Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/number-of-ads-per-ad-set/)).

**What changed.** Jon Loomer documents that **the quote no longer exists on the page** — Meta
"has softened the claim that reducing ad volume improves performance", retaining only "when an
advertiser runs too many ads at once, each ad delivers less often", and Meta "now openly
recommends **creative diversification**". Loomer ties the change to the **Andromeda** rollout —
infrastructure built precisely to handle far more creative variations
([Jon Loomer, "No More Six Ad Limit Per Ad Set"](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/no-more-six-ad-limit-per-ad-set/)).
Meanwhile Advantage+ Shopping goes the other way entirely: Meta recommends importing **all**
eligible ads so it can test "up to **150 creative combinations**" [snippet-only].

**So: the "3–5" number has no first-party source, present or past.** The nearest first-party
figure was 6-or-fewer, and it has been withdrawn.

**The strongest evidence-based framing found instead is a constraint, not a count.**
- **The binding constraint is spend per creative, not creative count.** AdExchanger: uploading
  "20 or more creatives per ad set" backfires because "**when creative supply dramatically
  outweighs available budget, learning slows, delivery fragments**"; upper-funnel tolerates
  more concepts, lower-funnel wants "fewer concepts and more performance-oriented variants"
  ([AdExchanger on Andromeda](https://www.adexchanger.com/data-driven-thinking/what-metas-andromeda-update-actually-changes-and-what-it-doesnt/)).
- **Ads inside an ad set do not get equal spend** — delivery concentrates on whichever ad the
  system predicts best, so extra ads may accumulate no data at all.
- **Launch simultaneously, not incrementally**, to avoid learning-phase resets — if you have
  three images × two headlines, launch all six at once in the same ad set.
- **Base rates are brutal.** Brkfst.io's own data across "hundreds of Meta ad accounts": "only
  **2% of creatives actually become winners**", and the right count depends on spend volume,
  conversion volume and creative cost — they decline to give a universal number
  ([Brkfst](https://www.brkfst.io/how-many-ads-should-you-actually-be-testing-on-meta/)).
- Published playbooks that *do* give numbers assume budgets most brands are below (e.g. "at
  least 2 creative concept tests and 6–12 different ads every week" for $30K–$100K/month
  accounts, with 20–30% of spend on testing).

**Where the "different framework or angle" half IS supported — and it is the stronger half.**
Meta's own diversification/iteration distinction (§6.5) says varying only the CTA or headline
is **iteration, not diversification**; genuine diversification varies visual + messaging +
format and is tailored to different personas/use-cases. So *if* N variations are produced, Meta
is on record that they must differ on more than the hook to count. **The claim's qualitative
half has first-party backing; its numeric half has none.**

### 7.4 Claim: PAS's short-form fit turns on the ~125-character Meta primary-text fold

**Verdict: the fold is real and well-documented, but it is not what makes PAS short-form.**

**The fold is real.** Meta's own Feed image-ad spec recommends **primary text 50–150
characters** and **headline 27 characters**
([Meta Ads Guide — Facebook Feed image](https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/image/facebook-feed)).
Practitioner spec sheets converge: primary text accepts up to ~500 characters but **truncates
at ~125 behind "See more" on mobile**; Meta does not reject the longer text, it silently
truncates the rendered preview
([AdsUploader Meta ad copy specs](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ad-copy-specs),
[CaptionSnap 2026 Feed limits](https://captionsnap.io/meta/facebook-feed-character-limits-2026),
[AdPlus 125-char explainer](https://adplus.com/tools/ad-specs-validator/facebook-125-character-primary-text)).
**Caveat: the widely-quoted "only ~1% of users click See More" figure is C-tier and no primary
source for it was found.** The fold's existence is well-sourced; its exact behavioural cost is
not.

**But the causation runs the other way.** PAS was short-form-friendly for decades before Meta
existed — its fit comes from having **only three beats, none of which requires an asset**
(no testimonial, no credential, no story, no offer detail). Compare: PASTOR needs six beats
including proof; QUEST needs five; the VSL needs ten to twelve. **Beat count and asset
dependency are what determine short-form fit; the 125-char fold merely sets where the penalty
lands.**

Two consequences worth carrying forward:
- The fold is **not PAS-specific**. It rewards any flow whose first beat can stand alone and
  earn the expand — which is exactly Brunson's Hook, Fortin's Qualify, RMBC's problem-mechanism
  line, and a single F→A→B chain, as much as PAS's P.
- **The fold argues for a first-beat rule, not a framework choice.** Whatever flow is chosen,
  the operative constraint is: *the first ~125 characters must do a complete job on their own.*

## 8. Two more pieces of canon the ticket named

### 8.1 Ogilvy's research-led doctrine

**Tier B** ([*Ogilvy on Advertising* quotes](https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/627791),
[Ryan Delaney's book notes](https://www.ryandelaney.co/book-notes/ogilvy-on-advertising-david-ogilvy),
[Confessions — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confessions_of_an_Advertising_Man)).

**What it claims.** "I do not regard advertising as entertainment or an art form, but as a
medium of information… I want you to find it so interesting that you buy the product." The
prescribed sequence: **do your homework** (study the product exhaustively, plus competitors and
prior campaigns) → **positioning** (bluntly: *what the product does, and who it is for*; the
SAAB-in-Norwegian-winters case) → **brand image/personality** (consumers choose brands, not
products; consistency is the asset) → **the big idea** ("you can do homework from now until
doomsday, but you will never win fame and fortune unless you also invent big ideas") →
**headline discipline** ("on the average, five times as many people read the headline as read
the body copy… you have spent eighty cents out of your dollar") → **long copy sells**
(readership drops sharply to ~50 words but little between 50 and 500 — hence the 607-word
Rolls-Royce ad) → **test everything; direct response is the truth-teller.**

Ogilvy's five-point big-idea checklist: *Did it make me gasp when I first saw it? Do I wish I
had thought of it myself? Is it unique? Does it fit the strategy to perfection? Could it be
used for 30 years?*

**Inputs.** Product dossier; competitive campaign history; prior response data; a defined
target; the existing brand image. The model presumes a **measurement loop**.
**Output.** A positioning statement + a big idea + a brand-personality spec, expressed as a
headline-led, benefit-explicit execution — plus a testing plan.

**Failure modes.**
- **Era-bound medium rules.** Most concrete rules are print rules; *Confessions* barely covers
  television and predates all digital media.
- **Long copy is genuinely contested.** The sharpest critique was published on **Ogilvy's own
  Medium publication**, calling the Rolls-Royce copy pride "dated to the point of irrelevancy"
  and arguing he'd "better fit those 607 words into 140 characters"
  ([Ogilvy on Digital Advertising](https://medium.com/ogilvy-on-digital-advertising/david-ogilvy-s-greatest-flaw-and-an-abbreviated-evolution-of-copy-3fd69a0b432)).
  The counter-camp is not weak ([VeryGoodCopy](https://www.verygoodcopy.com/verygoodcopy-blogs-7/long-copy-vs-short-copy)).
  **Copyhackers' resolution is the most useful: length is set by awareness stage, not by a
  universal rule** — which folds the dispute back into Schwartz.
- **Weak evidence base for the specific numbers.** The "five times / 80 cents" figure and the
  50-vs-500-word curve are Ogilvy's own 1963 assertions; **no independent modern replication
  was found.** Widely repeated as fact, single-sourced in reality.
- **Survivorship/authority bias** — the rules are drawn from his own wins, defended by his own
  account of them.

**Classification: hybrid GENERATOR + STRUCTURE, with a thin diagnostic front porch.** Generator
dominates ("homework → big idea" is explicitly a method for finding something to say). Structure
second (headline-then-body, benefit and brand name in the headline, long factual copy). The
diagnostic element is *offloaded to testing* rather than supplied as a taxonomy — which is the
precise complement to Schwartz, who has no generator.

### 8.2 The evidence-based counter-tradition (the field's biggest disagreement)

The direct-response canon above is a **persuasion-and-differentiation** tradition. There is a
substantial, better-evidenced school that disputes its premises, and a decision session should
know it exists even if it decides the brand's stage doesn't warrant acting on it.

- **Rosser Reeves, USP (*Reality in Advertising*, 1961)** — three rules: each ad makes a
  proposition ("buy this product and you will get this specific benefit"); the proposition must
  be one competitors cannot or do not offer; it must be strong enough to move mass millions
  ([Wikipedia/USP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unique_selling_proposition)).
- **Ehrenberg-Bass (Byron Sharp) reject the differentiation premise.** Their position:
  **distinctiveness** (recognisable brand assets, mental availability) beats **differentiation**
  (unique product claims); "customers don't care" about product differences and instead buy
  brands they remember and can find; success comes from **salience**, not positioning
  ([Ehrenberg-Bass, differentiation vs distinctiveness](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/differentiation-versus-distinctiveness)).
- **Binet & Field (IPA, 996 campaigns, 1980–2010)** — the **60/40** brand-building /
  sales-activation split ([Alex Murrell's summary](https://www.alexmurrell.co.uk/summaries/les-binet-and-peter-field-the-long-and-the-short-of-it)).
- **And they disagree with each other.** Byron Sharp has attacked the 60:40 rule as based on
  flawed data; Mark Ritson has attacked Ehrenberg-Bass's distinctiveness-over-differentiation
  framing as "consulting, dogma, ideology"
  ([Ehrenberg-Bass on Sharp vs Binet](https://marketingscience.info/prof-byron-sharp-skewers-binet-tells-marketers-to-sack-agencies-preaching-share-of-voice/),
  [Ritson in Mi3](https://www.mi-3.com.au/30-08-2023/dont-call-it-science-call-it-what-it-really-consulting-dogma-ideology-mark-ritson-why)).
- **Cialdini's seven principles** (reciprocation, commitment/consistency, social proof,
  authority, liking, scarcity, + **unity** from *Pre-Suasion*, 2016) sit alongside as a
  persuasion **checklist/generator**; the core six are reported to have largely survived the
  replication crisis, though that assessment is itself practitioner commentary
  ([Forbes/Dooley](https://www.forbes.com/sites/rogerdooley/2024/05/14/robert-cialdinis-principles-of-influence-have-held-up-for-40-years-heres-why/),
  [CXL on unity](https://cxl.com/blog/cialdini-unity/)).

**Why it matters here.** The direct-response canon and the evidence-based school are not
reconcilable by picking carefully — they disagree about whether a differentiated claim is what
grows a brand at all. The DR canon's own strongest empirical footing is that it is *measured*
(Ogilvy's point about direct response being the truth-teller); the academic literature's
verdict on the hierarchy-of-effects models it rests on is that **empirical support is weak**
(§3.1). Any doctrine record should state which side it is standing on, and why.

**Adjacent canon worth naming but not surveyed in depth:** Robert Collier's "always enter the
conversation already taking place in the customer's mind"; Joseph Sugarman's "slippery slide"
(every element's job is to get the next element read); John Caples' *Tested Advertising
Methods* headline discipline; Claude Hopkins' *Scientific Advertising*; the account-planning
tradition's **single-minded proposition** (Stephen King's T-Plan at JWT / Stanley Pollitt at
BMP, both 1968 — [APG](https://www.apg.org.uk/single-post/2001/04/02/what-is-account-planning-and-what-do-account-planners-do-exactly)),
which is the direct ancestor of the modern creative brief.

## 10. Disagreement register — where sources genuinely conflict

1. **AIDA's inventor.** Lewis 1898 (conventional) vs Dukesmith/Sheldon (Iwamoto 2023, who calls
   the Lewis hypothesis "unsupported or based on weak evidence"). §3.1.
2. **Does the hierarchy-of-effects family work at all?** Practitioner canon assumes yes; the
   academic review literature (Vakratsas & Ambler; MSI) finds little empirical support. §3.1.
3. **Long copy.** Ogilvy's own agency publication calls it obsolete for digital; working
   copywriters defend it; Copyhackers resolves it by making length a function of awareness. §8.1.
4. **Which "4 Ps"?** Picture-first (Hoke) vs Promise-first (cold-email variant) vs
   Problem/Promise/Proof/Proposal (Ray Edwards, which Copyhackers *prefers* to avoid seeming
   "scammy"). Substantive, not cosmetic. §5.1.
5. **PASTOR's letters.** Edwards' S and T are each two-part; third-party retellings routinely
   drop Story and Testimony. §2.3.
6. **The VSL step count.** 7 vs 10 (Edwards' own) vs 12 (DigitalMarketer). "Follow the VSL
   formula" is not reproducible without pinning the version. §5.4.
7. **Meta's Performance 5 has two first-party versions** that disagree on two of five items,
   and rename a third. §6.5.
8. **Meta's before/after weight-loss imagery rule** differs between the Transparency Center page
   and the help-centre restatement. Resolve conservatively: treat as prohibited. §6.5.
9. **Meta's ad-volume guidance reversed itself.** "6 or fewer creatives per ad set" was
   published, then removed; Meta now recommends creative diversification, and ASC tests up to
   150 combinations. §7.3.
10. **Meta's "one variable" A/B doctrine vs Meta's "vary visual + message + format
    simultaneously" diversification doctrine** are in direct tension. §6.5.
11. **Sound-off vs sound-on.** Meta's 2016–19 pages and its current Reels guidance say opposite
    things and both are live. §6.5.
12. **Does format matter?** Meta lists it as one of three mandatory diversity axes; Dara Denney
    says "format is overrated — the creator/talent and the messaging angle matter more." §6.5,
    §6.4.
13. **Is "angle" above or below "concept"?** Motion nests angle inside the concept; Denney and
    Levinger treat the emotional angle as the root with concept as its execution. No agreed
    vocabulary exists — a pipeline must define its own. §1.5, §6.4.
14. **Do iterations still work post-Andromeda?** Motion — the framework's own popularisers —
    now concede Andromeda has "reduced the effectiveness of iterations." §1.5.
15. **Volume doctrine.** Practitioner volume maximalism vs AdExchanger's "creative supply must
    match budget, or learning slows and delivery fragments." §7.3.
16. **Brunson's Hook (maximise stopping) vs Fortin's Qualify (filter wrong-fit out)** are
    opposite instructions for the same 125 characters. §5.2.
17. **Differentiation vs distinctiveness** — Reeves/Ogilvy/DR canon vs Ehrenberg-Bass; and
    Sharp vs Binet & Field vs Ritson inside the evidence-based camp. §8.2.
18. **Awareness→formula mapping** (the ticket's canonical mapping) has at least two
    incompatible C-tier versions and no primary source at all. §7.1.

## 11. Attribution corrections this sweep produced

Six things a doctrine doc would get wrong if it took the common phrasings on trust:

1. **"Makepeace's six leads" → Masterson & Forde, *Great Leads* (2011).** Makepeace is cited
   *inside* the book; **zero** sources support his authorship.
2. **"The 12-step VSL (Jim Edwards)" → DigitalMarketer's 12-step.** Edwards' own is **10 steps**,
   and he publishes a separate PAS-based VSL template.
3. **"ACCA (a copywriting formula)" → Colley's DAGMAR (1961)**, an advertising-goals
   measurement model. Bly catalogued it.
4. **"Star–Story–Solution (Robert Collier)" → unattributed.** Collier is thematic ancestry only.
5. **"FABV — V for Verification/proof" → V is Value.** No source supports the proof reading.
6. **"SLAP" and "the Five Objections" have no originator at all** (tier D), and SLAP's four
   letters name funnel events, not writing beats.

Also worth flagging as *not established*: PAS's originator (Bencivenga vs Kennedy, neither
documented), BAB's originator (Bly, unverified), the 4 C's exact wording on Bly's own site, and
the "80% of an ad's success is headline + lead" figure (AWAI marketing claim, no study found).

## 12. Open gaps this sweep did not close

- **Vietnamese-language canon and the local regulatory text.** Nghị định 15/2018 and the
  advertising law's restrictions on thực phẩm bảo vệ sức khỏe / meal-substitute claims were
  named but not read. That is a real constraint layer and deserves its own pass — it is
  stricter than Meta's policy in places.
- **The Vietnamese diet-market teardown** — deliberately out of scope here; it is
  [`02-market-teardown.md`](../issues/02-market-teardown.md). It is what will settle the
  sophistication-stage reading that §5.3 asserts (stage 4+) on inference.
- **No independent efficacy evidence exists for any copy framework in this document.** Not one.
  Every efficacy claim traces to its author's own testimony, its publisher's marketing, or a
  vendor's proprietary dataset. The only frameworks with published, third-party-auditable
  evidence behind them are the ones in §8.2 that *dispute* the DR tradition. Any decision here
  is a judgement about craft, not a reading of evidence, and the doctrine record should say so.
